    Sources

    Open databases

    Open Food Facts and Open Beauty Facts

    GreenScan checks these open product databases first when a barcode is scanned. Their product data is available under open data licenses, including the Open Database License.

    Open Food Facts Open Beauty Facts ODbL License

    Food reference

    USDA FoodData Central

    GreenScan can use USDA FoodData Central as a public nutrition reference for food and drink scoring logic, especially nutrient context such as sugar, sodium, fat, and serving data when available.

    FoodData Central USDA API guide

    Cosmetic reference

    EU CosIng and Cosmetic Ingredient Review

    GreenScan uses public cosmetic ingredient references to improve ingredient names, functions, and restriction notes. These sources help us flag potential cosmetic concerns, but they do not replace a professional safety assessment.

    EU CosIng Cosmetic Ingredient Review

    Chemical reference

    NIH PubChem

    GreenScan can use PubChem as a public chemistry reference for ingredient name matching, CAS-style lookup, and hazard context. PubChem is used carefully as a reference source, not as a live request for every scan.

    External education

    EWG Skin Deep

    GreenScan may link to EWG Skin Deep as an outside educational resource, but GreenScan does not scrape EWG, copy EWG product scores, or use EWG scores as our scoring system without permission.

    Scanner technology

    Browser scanner with open-source fallback

    GreenScan first tries the browser's built-in barcode reader when supported. If that is unavailable or unstable, it falls back to open-source JavaScript barcode scanning so iPhone, iPad, Android, and desktop browsers have a better chance of reading UPC and EAN codes.

    Barcode Detection API ZXing JS Quagga2

    Enrichment policy

    Caching before live lookups

    When GreenScan adds reference enrichment, it should cache ingredient and product results in our saved database first. This helps avoid repeated API calls, protects rate limits, and keeps scans fast.

    AI fallback

    When no product match is found

    If a barcode is not found in the open databases, GreenScan asks for product photos and uses AI to read the ingredient label, rate the product, and save that barcode result in our database so future scans can load it without using AI again.

    Offline behavior

    Known products work offline, new labels queue

    When you do not have internet, GreenScan can still open the app shell and load products already saved on your device. New ingredient photos, front-label photos, pasted ingredients, and nutrition labels can be saved into a small on-device queue. When you reopen GreenScan with internet, queued items are analyzed once, deduplicated by barcode and label fingerprint, and then added to the shared database when possible.

    Your AI key

    Optional personal provider

    Signed-in users can add their own ChatGPT, Anthropic, or Google AI API key in Settings. This is optional; GreenScan provides AI by default, but using your own key gives you more control over your own analysis usage. GreenScan daily AI limits do not apply when your own key is used.

    Open source

    GitHub users bring their own API keys

    The GreenScan source code is available on GitHub for review and self-hosting. If you clone or deploy your own copy, you must configure your own Cloudflare account, OpenAI or other AI provider key, helper services, and usage limits. GreenScan's production API keys, credits, and free trial usage are not included or shared with GitHub copies.

    User data

    History sync

    If you sign in with Google, GreenScan saves your recent scan history and recent searches on our servers so your account can restore them across devices. Guests keep history on their phone or browser only. Product photos are not saved in account history as large inline images.

    GreenScan may also save shared barcode ingredient results so the same product can load later without using AI again.

    Privacy

    What GreenScan stores

    Google sign-in is used to identify your account email and restore account features. GreenScan does not sell personal scan history. Signed-in history is limited to recent product scan data, and you can clear it from History. AI ingredient photos are processed to create product data; large personal photo data is not stored in account history.

    Safety vocabulary

    Flagged ingredient list

    GreenScan's local vocabulary includes ingredients and additive categories flagged by public regulatory sources, including FDA food and cosmetic restrictions and EU food/cosmetic safety actions. When an ingredient is banned, limited, restricted, no longer authorized, or warned about in those sources, GreenScan shows that note in the ingredient details. The vocabulary is used as a screening guide, not medical advice.

    Important note

    Educational screening only

    GreenScan is an educational ingredient screening tool. Scores and ingredient notes are not medical advice, diagnosis, allergy advice, or a guarantee that a product is safe or unsafe for every person. If you have allergies, sensitivities, medical conditions, pregnancy concerns, or questions about a product, check the package label and ask a qualified professional.

    Scoring method

    How GreenScan scores products

    Every product starts at 100. GreenScan subtracts points for potential concerns found in the ingredient list: potential high risk -18, potential moderate risk -8, and unknown concern -3. If no ingredient list is available, the score is lowered because the result has less evidence.

    Food and drink products can lose extra points for added sugar, flagged additives, added fats or oils, artificial colors, high fructose corn syrup, BHA/BHT, sodium nitrite, high sugar, high fat, high saturated fat, high sodium, or a poor Nutri-Score. Beauty and hair products can lose extra points for fragrance, strong sensitizers, formaldehyde releasers, drying alcohols, or sulfates.

    Products can gain small bonuses for no high-concern ingredients, shorter ingredient lists, whole-food signals, no added sugar, no flagged additives, no added fats or oils, or beauty formulas without fragrance and common sensitizers. Score ranges: 90-100 Excellent, 75-89 Good, 50-74 Fair, 25-49 Poor, and 0-24 Bad.
